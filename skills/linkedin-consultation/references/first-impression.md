# First impression: URL, photo, cover, location, headline

> Your profile starts working before the recruiter even clicks on it. It starts in the search
> results, now with AI more than ever before.

## URL

If the link looks like `linkedin.com/in/john-doe-7a921b123`, it signals either inexperience or
indifference. A clean URL takes two minutes and is permanently visible everywhere the link
appears: email signatures, CVs, business cards.

**Always present several options and let the user choose.** Adapt the keyword to what is actually
relevant for them. For a Product Manager named Jana Nováková:

- `jana-novakova` — clean, minimal
- `jana-novakova-product-manager` — role spelled out in full
- `jana-novakova-product` — domain spelled out
- `novakova-product-manager` — surname-first variant

**Two hard constraints:**

- **No diacritics.** LinkedIn URLs must be ASCII-safe. Convert: Žužana Pešková → `zuzana-peskova`.
- **No abbreviations.** `john-doe-pm` is not industry-agnostic: PM reads as Product Manager,
  Project Manager or Production Manager depending on who is looking. If the user already has an
  abbreviation in their URL, flag it and explain the ambiguity. Spell the role or domain out, or
  fall back to the clean name baseline.

**Push level, by tier:**

| Current state | Verdict | How hard to push |
|---|---|---|
| `john-doe-7a921b123` | Must-fix | Flag clearly |
| `john-doe` | Correct baseline | Acknowledge it, then explain that a keyword could raise the chance of being found, by recruiters and AI sourcing tools alike. Ground the argument in their target role, industry and seniority. Convince through reasoning, don't push. |
| `john-doe-engineer` | Nice-to-have | Mention as an option, no pressure |

## Photo

> This isn't Instagram, but it isn't a passport office either. The goal is approachable and
> professional: look like the person the recruiter is going to meet on the video call. If the
> photo is years old, that gap shows up in the first 30 seconds of an interview.

Benchmark: chest-up, neutral or simple background, good lighting, face clearly visible. Corporate
but relaxed. This works across industries and seniority levels.

**What to look for:** Is the face clearly visible and well lit? Is the background clean and
non-distracting? Is the framing chest-up? Does the overall impression match the professional
context they're targeting?

**Mood shots, artistic and heavily stylised photos:** flag directly, these aren't right for a
professional network regardless of aesthetic quality. Then calibrate the fix urgency to role and
industry. A creative director or UX designer has latitude a lawyer or finance professional does
not: worth reconsidering in the first case, must-fix in the second.

**Explain why it matters**, whether flagging a problem or confirming a good photo: it's among the
first things seen, it sets the tone before a word is read, and on mobile it dominates the screen.
An outdated photo creates a gap between expectation and reality, which is friction before the
conversation has started.

If the image is too small or too low-resolution to judge, ask for a closer one before commenting.

## Cover picture

Treat it as a free ad banner. Not using it is a wasted opportunity.

- Avoid generic stock: office buildings, hands typing on a laptop.
- Use it as social proof: speaking at a conference, running a workshop, presenting. That signals
  value immediately.
- A short text overlay works: value proposition, certifications (AWS, Google Certified), or role
  plus impact.
- On mobile the profile photo overlaps more of the cover than on desktop. Compose for that.

## Location

Accuracy is essential. It must match where the user actually wants to be found, or be set to
Remote if that's the goal. Location filters use this field directly.

## Headline

The headline is the punchline. It's also the primary text keyword-scraped by recruiting tools.

> 🛑 Reality check: in my experience a recruiter spends only a couple of seconds on the headline
> before deciding to scroll or bounce. Tell the world what you do, not what you want or believe.
> Be specific, use keywords, and consider symbols and emoji for legibility.

Aim to carry the keywords in roughly the first 120 characters, since the headline truncates in
search results and feed views. A guideline for where attention lands, not a platform limit.

**Generic against specific:**

| Role | Too generic | Impact plus keywords |
|---|---|---|
| Developer / Engineer | Full Stack Developer | Senior Full Stack Engineer |
| | Application Developer | TypeScript Software Engineer |
| Product | Product Manager | Senior Product Manager @ B2B SaaS |

The pattern across the right-hand column: seniority, specialisation, or the market segment
worked in. Something a search filter can match that "Product Manager" alone cannot.
