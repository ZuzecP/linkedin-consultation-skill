# Drafting and copywriting rules

Load this before writing or rewriting any section.

## Before the first draft

1. **Establish the target audience.** Role type, industry, company type (startup or enterprise,
   B2B or B2C). If the user can't define it, help them: "Who do you want to find you? What kind
   of role, in what kind of company?" Everything downstream depends on this answer.
2. **Establish the copy language.** Ask which language the profile copy should be written in.
   This is independent of the language the consultation itself is happening in; someone may be
   consulting in Czech and writing their profile in English.

## While drafting

3. **Work in small units.** One section at a time, never the whole profile at once.
4. **Draft, then iterate.** Present a draft, ask what resonates and what doesn't, revise.
5. **Never invent a fact.** Missing a team size, a metric, a tool? Ask for it. Don't estimate,
   don't assume, don't round up. If the user says "just make something up," decline and explain
   why: fabrication in a LinkedIn profile is a liability, not an asset, and it surfaces in the
   first interview.
6. **Confirm before finalising.** "Does this accurately reflect your experience?" before calling
   a section done.

## Length guardrails

Working targets, not hard rules. LinkedIn shows a live character counter in the field itself,
which is the authority; tell the user to check it when pasting.

| Field | Working target |
|---|---|
| Headline | ~220 characters |
| About | ~2,600 characters |
| Experience description, per role | ~2,000 characters |

Check a draft against these before presenting it. Being near the limit is not a goal in itself:
a headline that uses 90 characters well beats one padded to 220.

Separately, and for a different reason: aim to carry the headline's keywords in roughly the
first 120 characters, because that's where attention lands before truncation in search results
and feed views.

## Tone

Match the user's own voice, not a LinkedIn house style. If their answers in conversation are
plain and specific, the copy should be plain and specific. Resist the pull toward "passionate
about driving impact" phrasing; it's the default the whole methodology exists to avoid.
